Overview

These four patents cover a family of inventions in which a display system observes something about a viewer and changes the image shown in response. The input channel varies across the patents: gaze direction, voice, and facial appearance each serve as the triggering condition for image updates. The claims address both the detection logic and the image-modification system that acts on it.

The first patent was filed in March 2016. The family expanded through a continuation and two new applications, each targeting a distinct modality. All four were examined and allowed at the USPTO under Class G06F 3, which covers input interfaces between humans and computers, specifically the detection of eye movements, gestures, and related physiological signals.

I was a co-inventor on all four, alongside my father, brothers, and two collaborators. The work grew out of a shared interest in how physical spaces and the people in them can interact with one another through a display as the intermediary.

US 9,990,035 B2 Granted

Image Changes Based on Viewer's Gaze

This is the original filing in the family. The invention describes a display system that tracks where a viewer is looking and updates the image shown on the display in response to that gaze data. The core claims cover the combination of an eye-tracking input mechanism and a controller that translates detected gaze position into instructions for changing the displayed image.

At the time of filing, eye-tracking technology was becoming more accessible in consumer hardware, and the patent addressed a concrete application of that capability: using gaze not just as a passive metric but as an active input that drives what the viewer sees. The prosecution took roughly two years from filing to grant, with the USPTO classifying the invention under G06F 3/013, the subclass for interfaces that recognize eye position and movement.

US 10,416,765 B2 Granted

Image Changes Based on Viewer's Gaze (Continuation)

Filed the same month the original gaze patent was granted, this continuation pursues claim coverage on aspects of the invention that were not fully captured by the first application. A continuation shares its priority date with the parent, which means it benefits from the earlier 2016 effective filing date when evaluating prior art.

The continuation strategy here is fairly standard: once a parent patent issues, the applicant has a window to file a continuation and direct the claim scope toward features or variations that were disclosed but not yet claimed. This patent was granted in September 2019, about 15 months after filing, a faster prosecution cycle than the original.

US 11,016,563 B2 Granted

Image Changes Based on Voice

This application extended the core concept to a second input modality. Rather than gaze, the triggering condition here is voice: the system detects spoken input from the viewer and uses it to determine how the displayed image should change. The claims address the voice detection system, the logic that maps audio input to image update instructions, and the display control mechanism that carries those instructions out.

Filed two years after the continuation, this is a new application rather than a continuation, so it carries its own priority date of September 2019. It was granted in May 2021, roughly 20 months after filing. The prosecution record at the USPTO sits under Application No. 16/572,315.

Image Changes Based on Facial Appearance

The most recent granted patent applies the same adaptive display concept to facial appearance as the input. The system uses computer vision to detect characteristics of the viewer's face and uses those characteristics to select or modify the image on the display. This third modality, alongside gaze and voice, completes a framework in which several distinct channels of human presence and expression can each serve as the input to a responsive display.

The application was filed in July 2022 and granted in October 2023, a prosecution duration of about 15 months. The published application, US 2022/0350405 A1, is available in the USPTO's public file and reflects the claims as originally filed before examination.

Classification

CPC Subclasses Applied

The USPTO examiner assigned all four patents to subclasses of G06F 3, which covers input arrangements for interacting with computers. The specific subclasses reflect the dual nature of the inventions: detecting physiological signals on one side and translating them into interface commands on the other.

CPC Code Description Appears In
G06F 3/013 Detecting position or movement of the eye; recognizing eye-movement based input. The primary classification for the gaze-based patents. All four patents
G06F 3/011 Detecting the position or movement of a body part used as input. Covers proximity and presence detection aspects of the claims. US 9,990,035; US 11,016,563; US 11,782,507
G06F 3/04845 Scrolling or panning; navigating through displayed content. Appears in the continuation where gaze-driven content navigation is claimed. US 9,990,035; US 10,416,765; US 11,782,507
G06F 3/0487 Using special-purpose keys or combinations of keys as input. Covers multi-signal and compound-input aspects of the claim set. All four patents
